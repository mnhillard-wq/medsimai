Overview

What's in this course

The MedSimAI Demo Course (Course 58) contains 13 cases spanning outpatient primary care, the emergency department, inpatient medicine, psychiatry, OB/GYN, and pediatrics. This page orients faculty and learners to every case at a glance — the clinical skills practiced, the learning objectives, the activities a learner completes, and where each activity happens on the platform.

How to use this page Start with the Learning Progression map below to see how cases build in complexity. Each case card links down to a full detail entry with expandable sections for objectives, skills, the learner's step-by-step activity sequence, and faculty orientation notes.

Learner-level categories

🟢 MS1 Entry-Level Four foundational cases — Melanie Jones, Sean Muller, Jordan Fullerton, Marielena Gil — tagged MS1-MS2 on the platform. Each focuses on complete or focused history-taking, basic exam interpretation, and (in two cases) a first oral presentation.
🔵 Advanced Nine clerkship-level-or-beyond cases spanning MS3 clerkships (medicine, psych, OB/GYN, pediatrics) plus one cross-cutting, level-agnostic presentation-coaching tool.

Platform Orientation

How cases work on the platform

Every case has two views: a faculty-facing Manage view for authoring, and a learner-facing Practice flow for completing the encounter.

Manage view (6 tabs)

  1. Educational Intent
    Learner level, practice focus, learner challenge/gap, observable behavior, organ system tags, and the four-axis complexity rating (diagnostic reasoning, management, communication, information gathering).
  2. Patient Data
    Demographics, vitals, history, and physical exam findings the simulated patient can draw on.
  3. Activity Plan
    Defines each phase/step of the encounter — activity type, participant, door-note instructions, and feedback settings.
  4. Case Summary (primary source for this page)
    Chief concern, clinical one-liner, learning objectives, and the differential with supporting/against evidence.
  5. Rubric Details
    Grading rubrics attached to each required output (transcript, oral presentation, note).
  6. Publish Checklist
    Readiness check before a case goes live to learners.

Practice flow (learner-facing)

  1. Case overview & door note
    Patient identity, setting, and the specific task for this step.
  2. Chart review
    Available before and during the encounter — vitals, labs, prior notes.
  3. Encounter
    Voice or chat with the patient, a family member, and/or an attending, depending on the case's activity plan.
  4. Follow-up activity
    Oral presentation, note writing, or an orders/management interface, if the case includes one.
  5. Feedback
    Rubric-based scoring on history-taking, communication, presentation, and/or documentation.

Activity types referenced in this course

Activity typeWhat it involves
History & PhysicalLearner interviews the patient and performs/interprets a physical exam via chat or voice.
Clinical InterviewHistory-only encounter with no physical exam (used in Marielena Gil).
Oral Presentation & DiscussionStructured case presentation to an attending, followed by Q&A.
Clinical Reasoning DiscussionA more Socratic, less formal exchange with an attending — brief summary, differential, and plan, with probing follow-ups (Alex Rivera, Jordan Liu).
Orders / ManagementLearner enters diagnostic/treatment orders and reviews results as they evolve over a simulated timeline (Febrile Infant only).
