Overview

What this course is

This course helps you build confidence and rapport while taking a complete medical history in Spanish. The goal is not perfect fluency, but connecting with patients and gathering an organized history one step at a time.

It runs on MedSimAI, an online platform for practicing clinical encounters with AI-simulated patients. You interview a virtual patient by voice or text, just as you would in clinic, and receive structured feedback afterward — so you can rehearse taking a history as many times as you like, at your own pace.

Before you start

Preparation for every encounter

Two things to carry into all of your encounters: a mental map of what a complete history covers, and a few principles for caring well across languages.

Taking a complete history

  • Chief concern & HPI — why the patient came in, and the full story of the symptom.
  • Past medical & surgical history — prior conditions and operations, with details.
  • Medications & allergies — everything they take, and what they react to.
  • Family & social history — family illnesses and the patient's life, habits, and safety.
  • Review of systems — a quick head-to-toe screen for symptoms you have not covered.
Full complete-history checklist (click to expand — the elements you are building toward)
Chief concern & history of present illness
  • Location · Quality · Severity · Timing/chronology (how long, constant or intermittent)
  • Setting · Modifying factors (better/worse, anything tried) · Associated symptoms
Past medical & psychiatric history
  • Each condition; when and how it was diagnosed; how it was/is treated; any complications
Surgical history
  • Each surgery; when; why; any complications
OB/GYN history (patients assigned female at birth)
  • Age at first period (menarche); pregnancy history; deliveries and complications
Medications
  • Prescription medications with dose and frequency; problems taking or accessing them
  • Over-the-counter medications; supplements, herbal, or home remedies
Allergies
  • Any allergies, including food — and the specific reaction to each
Social, occupational & sexual history
  • Where born / where they live now; who they live with; social supports
  • Relationship/partner; safety at home (screen for violence); sexual history (asked sensitively)
  • Occupation; education / health literacy; tobacco, alcohol, recreational drugs; diet & exercise
Family history
  • Medical problems in parents, siblings, or children
Review of systems
  • Explain what the ROS is, move in an organized order, and screen ≥2 items per system
  • General · Skin · Neuro/Psych · HEENT · Endocrine · Cardiovascular · Pulmonary · GI · GU · Musculoskeletal · Hematologic

Language-concordant care

When you and your patient can speak directly in their preferred language — language-concordant care — communication is better and outcomes improve. A few principles keep that care safe and respectful:

  • Know your level, honestly. Being able to chat is not the same as being able to communicate health concepts. Even strong self-raters should keep training and be formally assessed.
  • You are the clinician, not the interpreter. If a task is beyond your Spanish, use a qualified medical interpreter rather than switching roles yourself.
  • Communicate to be understood. Check the patient's understanding, avoid jargon, and offer a simpler synonym when a word doesn't land. If you use an English term, say so and give the Spanish equivalent.
  • Lead with respect and privacy. Use a warm greeting and the respectful usted, and open sensitive topics with a privacy statement — "Estas preguntas son confidenciales y se las hago a todos mis pacientes."
  • Stay curious, not assuming. Ask about home and herbal remedies openly; they are a normal and important part of many patients' care.

Module 1 — Chief Concern & History of Present Illness

Case 2A
ObjectiveGreet the patient, build rapport, and elicit her chief concern and the full story of her present illness in Spanish.

The questions to master

  • Open the visit — greet her warmly, introduce yourself, and ask what brought her in. "¿Qué la trae hoy?"
  • Characterize the symptom across all seven attributes — location, quality, severity, and timing. "¿Dónde le duele?" · "¿Cómo es el dolor?" · "Del uno al diez, ¿qué tan fuerte es?" · "¿Cuándo empezó? ¿Es constante o va y viene?"
  • Cover setting, modifiers, and associated symptoms. "¿Hay algo que lo mejora o lo empeora?" · "¿Ha notado otros síntomas?"

Module 2 — Past Medical & Surgical History

Case 2A
ObjectiveGather her past medical and past surgical history in Spanish, transitioning smoothly from her presenting concern into her background.

The questions to master

  • Past medical & psychiatric problems — for each, get when and how it was diagnosed, how it is treated, and any complications. "¿Ha tenido alguna enfermedad? ¿Cómo se la diagnosticaron? ¿Cómo la han tratado?"
  • Surgical history — what, when, why, and any complications. "¿Le han hecho alguna cirugía u operación? ¿Cuándo y por qué?"
  • OB/GYN history may come up here — age at first period and any pregnancy history. "¿A qué edad tuvo su primera regla? ¿Ha estado embarazada?"

Module 3 — Medications, Allergies & Herbal Remedies

Case 2A
ObjectiveCover her medications, allergies, and use of herbal remedies in Spanish.

The questions to master

  • Medications — prescription and over-the-counter, with dose and frequency, plus any trouble taking or affording them. "¿Toma algún medicamento, con o sin receta? ¿Qué dosis y con qué frecuencia?"
  • Supplements & home remedies — vitamins, supplements, herbs, or remedios caseros. "¿Usa vitaminas, suplementos o remedios caseros?"
  • Allergies — including food, and the specific reaction to each. "¿Es alérgica a algún medicamento o comida? ¿Qué le pasa cuando lo toma?"

Module 4 — Family & Social History

Case 2B
ObjectiveObtain her family and social history in Spanish, including a sensitive sexual-health history introduced with a privacy statement.

The questions to master

  • Family history — health problems in parents, siblings, or children. "¿Alguien en su familia tiene problemas de salud?"
  • Social history — living situation and supports, school and work, and habits like tobacco, alcohol, and drugs. "¿Con quién vive? ¿A qué se dedica? ¿Fuma? ¿Toma alcohol?"
  • Safety & sexual health, asked sensitively — open with a privacy statement, then screen for safety and sexual health. "Estas preguntas son confidenciales… ¿Se siente segura en casa?"

Module 5 — Review of Systems

Case 2B
ObjectiveComplete a focused review of systems in Spanish, screening the body systems you have not yet covered.

The questions to master

  • Set it up — explain what the ROS is before you start, a quick set of screening questions. "Le voy a hacer unas preguntas rápidas sobre diferentes partes del cuerpo."
  • Move in an organized order and screen at least two items per system: general, skin, neuro/psych, HEENT, endocrine, cardiovascular, pulmonary, GI, GU, musculoskeletal, hematologic.
  • Keep her oriented — because the ROS is high-volume, avoid jargon, don't rush, and check in as you go.

Full-case run-through (recommended, not required)

Another module of sorts — recommended but optional. Bring it all together: conduct Marielena's complete history in one continuous Spanish encounter, from greeting and rapport through the review of systems, closing professionally.
