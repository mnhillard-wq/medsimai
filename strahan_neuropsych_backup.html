Learning objectives
  1. Obtain a clinical history for a patient with altered mental status, identifying what can be reliably obtained from the patient herself and what can be obtained from a frightened family member.
  2. Direct a focused physical examination, naming which findings would change the differential and why.
  3. Construct and defend a prioritized differential diagnosis for altered mental status, spanning both systemic and neurologic causes.

Learner instructions

You are a clinical learner in the emergency department. You will speak with two people: a patient, and then her mother. Plan on twenty to thirty minutes.

Everything the simulated patient says and does is intentional. If something sounds strange to you, treat it as a clinical finding rather than a technical fault. Listen to how she speaks as carefully as you listen to what she says.

You cannot see her, so you will have to ask for what you want. Request specific examination findings by typing the maneuver or test you are interested in, and you will be given the result. You can conduct a mental status examination simply by asking her questions. What you cannot do is stumble onto a finding, so decide what you want to know and why, then ask for it.

She may not be able to tell you what you need. Anything you cannot get from her, ask her mother in Step 2. Her mother knows a great deal more, though not every fact about her adult daughter's life. You are practicing real medicine here: the gaps are part of the case, not a fault in it.

After the encounter, draft the reflection at the bottom of this page and bring it to your debrief. Informal, not graded.
